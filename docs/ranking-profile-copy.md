# Octagon HQ Fighter Profile Writing Standard

**Status:** Canonical editorial memory  
**Reviewed:** 2026-08-17

Use this document as the source of truth whenever writing or reviewing ranked-fighter profile copy in Octagon HQ.

## Core structure

Every ranked fighter profile has three editorial sections:

1. **Overview** - Who is this fighter?
2. **Why Ranked Here** - What did this fighter accomplish in the UFC to earn this historical standing?
3. **Why Not Higher** - What genuinely limits the UFC case?

The profile should read like a sharp MMA assessment from someone who knows the fighter and the career, not an explanation of a ranking algorithm.

The three sections have different jobs. Avoid repeating the same evidence across them.

## Word-count bands

These ranges are hard targets based on the fighter's current ranking.

| Current ranking | Overview | Why Ranked Here | Why Not Higher |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1-5 | 34-40 | 50-60 | 45-60 |
| 6-15 | 30-38 | 45-55 | 40-55 |
| 16-30 | 27-35 | 40-50 | 35-50 |
| 31-50 | 24-32 | 35-45 | 32-45 |
| 51-82 | 20-28 | 30-40 | 30-40 |

Absolute limits:

- Overview: 20-40 words
- Why Ranked Here: 30-60 words
- Why Not Higher: 30-60 words

Rank determines the writing budget. Do not pad a fighter merely to reach the top of the range.

## 1. Overview

### Core question

> What is the first thing a knowledgeable UFC fan should think about when they hear this fighter's name?

This is the fighter's introduction. It is not simply a fighting-style paragraph and it is not a miniature resume summary.

Identify the one or two things most inseparable from the fighter's identity and build the Overview around them. Depending on the fighter, that can include:

- fighting style or signature weapons
- dominance
- aura or star power
- fame or cultural significance
- physical characteristics
- fight IQ or adaptability
- toughness or durability
- a defining career arc
- longevity
- unusual age-related achievement
- reinvention
- a defining controversy
- a specialist identity
- personality or showmanship when genuinely central to the UFC identity

Do not force every category into every fighter. Conor should sound like Conor. Glover should sound like Glover. Demian Maia should sound like Demian Maia.

### Numbers in the Overview

Resume statistics generally belong in Why Ranked Here. An exact number is allowed when the number itself is part of the fighter's identity.

Good example: becoming UFC champion at 42.

Less appropriate: listing UFC wins, Top-5 wins, and title-fight totals in the Overview.

### Opponent names

Opponent names should generally be avoided in the Overview. They belong primarily in Why Ranked Here or Why Not Higher.

An opponent should appear in the Overview only when the relationship or moment is genuinely inseparable from the fighter's identity, not merely because it was an important win.

### Career evolution

Mention evolution only when it is one of the first things that matters about the fighter. Do not mechanically summarize early career, prime, and late career for everyone.

### Fame, aura, and significance

Fame, aura, showmanship, and cultural significance are valid Overview material when they are genuinely central to how the fighter is understood. They do not need to apply to every fighter.

The Overview does not need to justify the ranking.

## 2. Why Ranked Here

### Core question

> What did this fighter actually accomplish in the UFC that makes this historical standing make sense?

This is the affirmative competitive case. Use the strongest evidence rather than everything available.

Typical priority:

**Championship success -> elite wins -> sustained excellence -> longevity -> exceptional achievements**

Relevant evidence can include:

- championships
- title-fight wins
- successful defenses
- championships across divisions
- elite opponents beaten
- ranked or Top-5 wins
- major win streaks
- sustained contender relevance
- historic performances
- unusual competitive achievements

### Names and numbers

This is where exact statistics and opponent names usually belong.

Prefer concrete evidence over vague praise, but do not turn the section into a database dump. Use the few strongest facts that explain the fighter's case.

### Fame and aura

Fame, drawing power, celebrity, aura, and cultural importance should not substitute for competitive evidence in Why Ranked Here unless the ranking philosophy is explicitly changed to score those things.

They can still be essential to the Overview.

### Other promotions

The ranking argument is based on UFC accomplishment. Pride, WEC, Strikeforce, Bellator, ONE, and regional achievements do not strengthen Why Ranked Here.

Broader significance may occasionally provide context in the Overview, but non-UFC accomplishments are not ranking evidence.

## 3. Why Not Higher

### Core question

> What are the few most important things genuinely preventing this UFC resume from being stronger?

This is the strongest intelligent counterargument to the fighter's case. It is not a requirement to invent criticism.

Only include drawbacks that materially matter.

### Relevant losses

Losses are especially meaningful when they:

- occurred during the fighter's meaningful prime
- ended or materially damaged a title reign
- came against the caliber of opponent the fighter needed to beat
- exposed a recurring competitive limitation
- interrupted an otherwise dominant elite run
- represent an important part of understanding the fighter's ceiling

Late-career and clearly post-prime losses should usually receive much less weight and often should not appear at all.

Do not treat every loss equally simply because it exists.

### Do not manufacture negatives

A positive achievement is not automatically a criticism because the fighter failed to accomplish even more.

For example, Jon Jones winning two heavyweight title fights is a positive. "Only two heavyweight wins" is not automatically a legitimate drawback to his overall UFC case.

Why Not Higher should identify actual weaknesses in the case, not convert the absence of hypothetical additional accomplishments into negatives.

### The top fighter

For the fighter at the top of the rankings, Why Not Higher effectively means:

> What keeps this case from being flawless or completely untouchable?

Do not invent a superior resume that does not exist merely to satisfy the section title.

## Controversies

Competitive controversies can matter in Why Not Higher.

Examples include drug-testing failures, overturned results, suspensions that materially interrupted championship activity, significant weight issues related to major fights, or other circumstances that directly affect the competitive record.

Personal or legal controversies should not automatically become ranking deductions. They may appear in the Overview when they are genuinely inseparable from the fighter's public UFC legacy, but they should not be presented as evidence that the competitive resume itself was weaker unless they directly affected it.

Jon Jones is the calibration case: broader controversies are part of understanding his legacy, while drug-testing history, suspensions, stripped titles, and the overturned Daniel Cormier result directly affect the championship story and can therefore appear in Why Not Higher.

## Active fighters

Evaluate active fighters using what they have actually accomplished now.

Do not project future achievements with language such as "could become," "on pace to," "likely to eventually," or "should add more defenses."

An active career may be mentioned when it directly explains why a limitation could still change, but use this sparingly. The profile evaluates the current resume, not a projected future resume.

## Repetition

Each important fact should normally have one primary home.

Example for Conor McGregor:

- Overview: UFC's biggest superstar with aura, showmanship, and lethal counterstriking.
- Why Ranked Here: Aldo, Alvarez, simultaneous two-division championship, elite wins.
- Why Not Higher: no defenses, inactivity, and losses that limited sustained elite success.

High-level concepts may carry across sections when necessary, but do not repeat the same statistic, opponent list, or accomplishment simply to fill space.

## Voice

Write like an informed MMA fan making a strong, fair argument.

The copy should be direct, specific, natural, debate-ready, and confident without exaggeration.

It should never sound like generated database commentary.

Avoid repeated framing devices across fighters. Do not routinely begin with phrases such as:

- "is remembered as"
- "was known for"
- "defined by"
- "his case is built on"
- "the resume is anchored by"

Any of those may occasionally fit. None should become a template. Sentence openings, rhythm, and structure should vary naturally between fighters.

## Forbidden ranking-system language

Consumer-facing profile copy should not expose internal ranking terminology.

Avoid language such as:

- score / scoring
- model
- calculation
- inputs
- ledger / prime ledger
- scored era
- quality tier
- penalty
- weighting
- adjustment

Translate the underlying information into normal MMA language.

## Additional language rules

- Never state the fighter's exact ranking inside these sections.
- Say "UFC," not "UFC-only."
- Use normal MMA terminology rather than internal engineering terminology.
- Use standard ASCII punctuation in stored profile copy.
- Do not use embellished resume-style prose merely because the fighter ranks highly.
- Do not repeat visible statistics unnecessarily when a more meaningful accomplishment makes the same point.
- Every factual statement must be supported by canonical fighter data or explicitly reviewed evidence.

## Approved-copy protection

Once a fighter has received an intentional editorial review, the approved presentation copy is protected.

A normal ranking refresh, fight-history update, era update, or judgment update must **not** silently rewrite presentation copy. In `v2RankingRoster.ts`, data-only fighter refreshes must go through the canonical `rankingDataRefresh(...)` helper, which preserves the fighter's entire existing `presentation` object by construction.

When new data materially changes a fighter's story:

1. Update the canonical ranking data.
2. Leave the existing presentation unchanged.
3. Intentionally review that fighter under this writing standard.
4. Change presentation copy only as part of that explicit profile-copy review.

All three editorial sections should be reconsidered during a review, but only sections that genuinely need changing should be rewritten.

Future tooling may flag stale copy or draft a proposed revision, but it must not publish rewritten profile prose without intentional review.

## Calibration anchors

These fighters establish the intended flexibility of the standard:

- **Jon Jones:** complete fighter + dominance + longevity + aura + controversy.
- **Glover Teixeira:** defining late-career story first, then fighting identity.
- **Conor McGregor:** fame, aura, and cultural significance are essential to the Overview.
- **Mackenzie Dern:** signature skill leads, with career progression added when it materially matters.
- **Demian Maia:** specialist identity can almost completely own the Overview when that truly is the fighter's defining story.

The goal is **consistent standards without standardized personalities**.

> Every fighter follows the same editorial rules. Every fighter should still sound like themselves.
