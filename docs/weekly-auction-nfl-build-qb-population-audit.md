# NFL Build a QB v2 — population audit

## Status

Working population pass only. No trait grades are changed or implied by membership.

This audit starts from the existing 60-QB Build a QB subject, the repository's broader 122-QB playable NFL quarterback universe, and an explicit missing-name check for recognizable modern quarterbacks that the existing 122 snapshot may not contain.

## Locked population philosophy

Population and grading are separate decisions.

A quarterback belongs because he is recognizable enough for the intended group and makes a legitimate Build a QB auction card. A quarterback's grade is determined later by his actual Arm, Accuracy, Processing, and Mobility. Membership never raises or lowers a trait grade.

Lower-tier quarterbacks are desirable when they are recognizable. The pool should not collapse into only Hall of Fame / elite-career names. A lower-overall quarterback can be especially useful when he has a legitimately standout trait.

Do not add a quarterback merely to manufacture a low, middle, or high rating. Do not remove a quarterback merely because his eventual ratings create an inconvenient distribution. Grade distribution is discovered after membership is locked.

## Era / recognizability curve

Use the quarterback's meaningful NFL era rather than rookie year mechanically when a career clearly straddles an era boundary.

- **Pre-2000:** highly selective. Only clearly great / iconic quarterbacks with strong recognition belong.
- **2000–2004:** very selective. Stars, major identities, and unusually useful recognizable trait profiles can make it.
- **2005–2009:** selective but broader. Recognizable good starters and memorable specialists may make it.
- **2010 onward:** full-speed subject population. Career greatness is not required; recognizable lower-tier starters, busts, and specialists are allowed when they create a legitimate trait decision.
- Recency affects membership only. It never changes a trait grade.

The thresholds are editorial, not quotas.

## Build a QB-specific membership rule

A quarterback should normally satisfy both:

1. **Recognition:** a normal NFL fan in the intended Octagon HQ group can reasonably identify the player.
2. **Trait usefulness:** the player has a real, defensible four-trait profile worth auctioning. This can mean one elite tool and major weaknesses elsewhere.

Examples of the intended lower-tier archetype include Jay Cutler (premium Arm despite lower overall placement) and modern recognizable quarterbacks whose career value may be modest but whose trait shape is distinctive.

## Obvious completeness rule

Do not assume the repository's current 122-QB historical-consensus universe is complete enough to define the v2 population by itself.

For example, recognizable modern trait subjects such as Justin Fields can be absent from that historical-consensus snapshot even though they are obvious Build a QB candidates. Before population lock, run a separate modern-completeness check against the canonical NFL QB registry and current recognition evidence.
