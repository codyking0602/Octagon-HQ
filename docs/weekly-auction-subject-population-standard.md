# Weekly Auction subject population standard

## Scope

This is the default population standard for every Weekly Auction subject unless a sport or subject has an explicit reason to differ.

## Recency curve

Recency affects **membership only**. It never changes an item's grade after that item makes the subject pool.

- **2000–2004:** very selective. Keep only items with a strong historical identity hook, major accomplishment, iconic player/game, or clearly elite quality.
- **2005–2009:** selective, but broader than 2000–2004.
- **2010 onward:** full-speed inclusion. Keep items whenever they clear the subject's normal quality and recognizability bar.

These are editorial curves, not quotas. Do not force a specific percentage, count, or distribution by era.

## Identity hooks

For older items, a strong identity hook can justify membership even when the raw resume is not elite. Examples include an iconic player, defining game, major upset, championship run, historically important team, or season that clearly launched an era.

The identity hook affects whether an item belongs. It does **not** increase the grade.

## Grading separation

Once an item is admitted, grade the actual subject performance on its merits. Do not apply an age bonus or age penalty. Older and newer items use the same subject-specific grading philosophy.

Each subject may have a different natural grade range and distribution. Do not force grades into common bands across unrelated subjects.

## Product rule

Population and grading are separate decisions:

1. **Membership:** Is this still a legitimate and recognizable auction card for the intended audience?
2. **Grade:** How good was the item at the thing the subject is actually ranking?

This standard applies to team seasons, player seasons, careers, defenses, position groups, and future comparable Weekly Auction subjects.
